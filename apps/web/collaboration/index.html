See who is here

Everyone on a board is a wanderer: a named cursor in their own colour, moving as they move.

Cursors, selections and faces

A wanderer's cursor carries their name, and whatever they have selected is outlined in the same colour, so it is obvious who is holding what. A row of faces says who is here right now; the owner wears a crown, and a click on a face says which role that person has.

Visitors on a share link get a name of their own, like Hare, Otter or Finch, for as long as that tab is open. A room of people who never signed in can still be talked about out loud.

Edits that merge, never clash

The document is a CRDT. Two people typing in one paragraph merge character by character, and work done offline is kept in your browser and merged when you reconnect. There is no conflict dialog and no version of the page that wins over anybody else's.

Ink is the one thing that arrives whole: a pen stroke reaches everyone when you lift the pen, while your cursor shows that you are drawing it.

Share it your way

A board is yours alone until you share it. Two separate decisions: whether the address is an address or a key, and who in particular you are letting in.

A link, for whoever you choose

General access is one setting. Restricted means only the people you add can open the board. Anyone with the link means exactly that, as a viewer or an editor, signed in or not. Replace the link whenever you like and the old one stops working.

From the same dialog you can send it to WhatsApp, Telegram, Facebook, LinkedIn or an email.

Invitations that respect the inbox

Invite anyone by email address. An address that already has an account is given access straight away and told by mail. An address without one is not mailed at all: you get a sign-up link to pass on yourself, and the board is waiting the moment they activate their account.

Meadow never mails an address nobody has confirmed. That restraint is what keeps the mail people are actually waiting for, the activation and reset links, out of spam folders.

Asking, when the door is shut

Somebody who reaches a board they cannot open sees a way forward rather than a wall: one click asks for access. The request is a record, not a key. It waits in the owner's share dialog, which checks for new ones every few seconds while it is open, and nothing opens until the owner says so.

Three roles, checked everywhere

Owner, editor and viewer. The role is worked out in one place on the server and checked on every request and again when the live connection opens, so what somebody can do never depends on which screen they are looking at.

A share link never lowers anybody: an owner who opens their own board through the link they just copied is still the owner.

Control that takes effect at once

A password in front of the door, a lock on the document, and a way to end any session anywhere.

A password in front of everyone

Put a password on a board and everybody is asked for it, the owner included. Nothing of the document is loaded behind the prompt, so there is nothing to see through, and changing the password sends everyone already inside back to it.

Forgotten it? The owner, and only the owner, can have a code mailed to them and trade it for a temporary password, which expires and asks to be replaced with a proper one.

Locks, and access you can take back

The owner's lock stops every edit, their own included, which is how you present a board without nudging anything. There is also a lock for your own tab alone, which nobody else sees.

Remove somebody and the board closes for them at once. The copy their browser had is cleared as they go, so a reload shows them nothing.

The owner has locked this glade. Nobody can edit it until they unlock it.

Every session, visible

Your profile lists every browser signed in to your account, with where it is and when it was last active, and the list updates as it changes. End one and that browser is locked out immediately, even if it is sitting idle.

Sign-in tokens rotate every time they are refreshed, and a stolen copy is caught the next time either copy is used.

Thirty days to change your mind

A deleted board goes to the trash, where its owner can restore it for thirty days with its history, its link and its members intact. Pages torn out of a lea come back the same way.
