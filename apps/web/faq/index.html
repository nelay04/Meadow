The basics

What is Meadow?

Meadow is an infinite canvas whiteboard where writing, diagramming and drawing share one endless surface. A paragraph, a shape, a sticky note and a pen stroke are all objects you can place, connect and restack anywhere, and everyone on a board sees every change as it happens.

What is an infinite canvas?

An infinite canvas is a workspace with no page edge and no fixed size. You pan and zoom instead of scrolling to the bottom of a document, and a diagram, a paragraph and a sketch can sit side by side at whatever scale suits them.

What are glades and leas?

A glade is a board: an open canvas for diagrams, notes and sketches. A lea is a glade printed as ruled paper, like a diary: you write on the lines, each page has its own subject and date, and pages can be added and torn out. Both are the same live document underneath, and a lea offers writing rather than the drawing tools.

Can I type in Indian languages on a Meadow board?

Yes. Meadow has phonetic input for thirteen languages of India, including Bengali, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia, Punjabi, Assamese, Nepali and Sanskrit. You type nomoskar and get নমস্কার, with no keyboard layout to install.

Does phonetic input send anything over the network?

Only the English letters of the word you are typing, to Google's input service, which is where the ranked suggestions come from. Nothing else on the page is sent. Phonetic input is off until you switch it on, and offline, Bengali and Assamese keep working by rule without it.

Can I install Meadow as an app?

Yes. On a phone, use Add to Home Screen; in a desktop browser, use the install button in the address bar. Meadow then opens in its own window with its own icon, and the installed app opens without a network. Signing in and loading a board you have not opened before still need a connection.

Working together

Can several people edit a Meadow board at the same time?

Yes. Meadow is live by default: you see other people's cursors, selections and edits as they happen. Two people typing in the same paragraph merge character by character rather than overwriting each other.

Does Meadow work offline?

Yes. Edits are stored in your browser and merged when you reconnect, so closing the laptop mid-thought does not lose work and does not force anyone to choose which version to keep.

Do the people I share with need an account?

Not always. When you turn on a board's link, anyone holding it can open the board, signed in or not, as a viewer or an editor, whichever you choose. You can also invite an email address: one with an account is given access straight away, and for one without, you get a sign-up link to pass on yourself.

Privacy and access

Who can see my boards?

Only you, until you share one. You can invite people by email address, turn on a link for viewers or editors, or add a password that everyone must enter, the owner included. Removing someone's access closes the board for them immediately.

How do I sign in?

With an email address and password, with GitHub, or with Google. All three land on the same account, matched by your verified email address, and a forgotten password can be reset.

Can I see where my account is signed in?

Yes. Your profile lists every browser holding a session, with its address and when it was last active. Terminate one and that browser is locked out immediately, even if it is sitting idle.

What happens if I delete a board by mistake?

It goes to the trash, where you can restore it for thirty days with its history, share link and members intact. Pages torn out of a lea can be restored the same way.

Licence and hosting

Is Meadow open source?

No. Meadow is source-available: the code is public on GitHub under the PolyForm Internal Use License 1.0.0. You may run and modify it for your own organisation's internal use, for example on your own server with Docker Compose, but you may not redistribute it.

Can I run Meadow on my own server?

Yes, for your organisation's internal use. Meadow runs with Docker Compose, and the source page explains what the licence allows and how to start it.
