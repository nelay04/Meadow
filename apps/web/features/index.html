Glades: a clearing with no edges

A glade is an open canvas for diagrams, notes and sketches. Think out loud and let it sprawl: pan and zoom instead of scrolling, and set a paragraph beside a diagram at whatever scale suits it.

Eight shapes, each on its own key

Rectangle, ellipse, diamond, parallelogram, triangle, trapezoid, polygon and cylinder, each a single keypress away. Every shape holds its own label, and the label fits the shape rather than its box, so the words in a diamond stay inside the diamond.

  • Pick how many sides a polygon has
  • Labels never clip at the edge
  • After drawing, the tool hands back to select, so you can adjust what you just made

Connectors that hold on

Draw an arrow from one object to another and it binds to the shape's true outline, so it follows when either end moves. Route it straight, curved or at right angles, bend it, re-aim either end, and give it a label that travels with it.

  • Connection dots appear as you hover a shape
  • A curve is stored as a curve, so it stays smooth at any zoom
Connector routing

Ink with five nibs, and help when you want it

Round, felt, chisel, brush and highlighter, each in four widths from fine to heavy. A stylus's pressure shapes the line; with a mouse, speed stands in for it, because a mouse reports no pressure at all.

The pen can also correct what you drew, in two degrees. Tidy turns a hand-drawn box, ellipse or arrow into the real object and keeps the look of your ink; Shapes makes the same object in the board's own style.

  • A hand-drawn arrow becomes a real connector, elbows included
  • Assist works with the round and felt nibs
  • Every stroke is one object and one undo step

Rich text and sticky notes, edited in place

Text is real rich text, typed straight onto the canvas and crisp at any zoom, in eight sizes from 12 to 64. Sticky notes are for quick thoughts, and each one carries the name of whoever wrote it.

  • Enter edits the selected text, and so does a double-click
  • Copied objects paste into a mail as their words, not as coordinates

Guides that lay it out for you

Drag near a neighbour and edges and centres line up, with a line drawn through both so you can see which one matched. Gaps even themselves out along a row, and while you resize, an edge snaps to a neighbour's so a column ends up one width.

The stack panel lists every object front to back. Drag a row, type a position or nudge it with the keyboard to put this one behind that one.

  • Marquee-select to move many objects at once
  • Undo, redo, duplicate, and copy and paste between glades and tabs

Leas: a diary on the same canvas

Kraft paper, ruled and waiting. Open a lea and start writing: the caret is already on the first line.

Write on the lines, not in boxes

A lea is a glade printed as ruled paper. Click a rule and write on it. The ruling matches the type exactly, so the words never drift off the lines, and the page keeps a comfortable width on any screen and runs on as far as you write.

Print it on vintage kraft, light or dark paper, or paper that follows the theme. The stock is your own preference in this browser, so even a viewer can choose how a page they can only read looks to them.

  • Spelling is marked as you write, with a switch to turn it off
  • The rail keeps to select and pan: a diary is for sentences

Pages with a subject and a date

Every page has its own subject and its own date, written the way a diary writes one, 28th May, 2026, and picked from a calendar of Meadow's own. The list of pages sits beside the paper, one line each, so you can see what is in the diary and turn straight to a page.

Tear a page out and it waits in the lea's own trash, showing how long it has left, until you restore it or let it go for good.

Type in thirteen Indian languages

Phonetic input works the way Google Input Tools does: type the sound of a word in English letters, pick it from the list under the caret, and keep writing. On glades and leas alike.

Spell it the way it sounds

Type nomoskar and Bengali candidates appear under the caret; Space, Enter, a number or a click puts one on the page. The language is your own setting, so two people on one lea can each write in theirs.

  • Off until you turn it on
  • Only the English letters of the word being typed are sent, to Google's input service, never the page
  • Offline, Bengali and Assamese keep working by rule, on the Avro scheme
  • For the others, the list says plainly when the service cannot be reached

On every glade and lea

What holds the rest together: work that survives a dropped connection, an app you can install, one theme across the site and the app, and a board list that finds things.

Works offline

Every edit is kept in your browser first. Close the laptop mid-thought or keep working on a train, and it merges when you reconnect, with no conflict dialog and no overwrite.

Installs like an app

Add it to a phone's home screen or install it from a desktop browser. The installed app opens without a network; signing in and opening a board for the first time still need one.

Light, dark or your system's

One switch, shared by this site and the app, and remembered in every open tab. A lea's paper is a setting of its own on top.

Find any board

Everything, Recent, Glades, Leas and Trash, narrowed to boards you own or boards shared with you, and searchable by name. Glades show a preview of their canvas.

Thirty days to change your mind

A deleted board waits in the trash with its history, link and members intact. Only its owner can delete it, and only its owner can bring it back.

Fast on crowded boards

Shapes go to the GPU as one instanced draw call however many there are, and arrows and ink get a pass each. At 5,000 objects a frame costs 3.5 ms of CPU, measured.

Keyboard shortcuts

Every tool has a key of its own. On a Mac, Ctrl is Cmd.
